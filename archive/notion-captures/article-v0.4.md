## Release position

The source presents v0.4 as the story behind the Council: why multiple models
were used, what the documented method looked like, and why disagreement was
more valuable than a single confident answer. It is historical editorial
material, not a claim that v0.4 is the current public article.

## Main lessons

- Text hides flow, branches, dead ends, and loops. A diagram can make them
  inspectable, but only if it does not erase uncertainty to look finished.
- ROY measures understanding returned for the words invested, not visual beauty.
- Mermaid makes structure text-native and cheap to revise. Mermaid syntax and a
  Mermaid AI platform are related but not interchangeable.
- The purpose-built drawing tool can still fail when it renders before the
  brief is complete. Speed is not the same thing as synthesis.

## Manual council method

The source describes a deliberately human-in-the-loop process:

1. **Fan out:** give the same brief to independent systems without cross-
   contamination.
2. **Compare:** inspect divergence side by side rather than rewarding the first
   answer or the apparent consensus.
3. **Adjudicate:** decide which differences reveal a real structural insight and
   which are noise.
4. **Synthesize:** assemble the strongest framing, loop logic, labels, and
   reductions into a hybrid that none of the originals contained.

The source calls this co-opetition. The models compete on the same brief while
contributing pieces to a shared result. Automated multi-model tools may hide
the disagreement behind a final composite; this method keeps the disagreement
visible because that is where the learning happens.

## Council roles

The source describes ChatGPT as a workbench, Claude as a structural analyst,
Perplexity as a grounded synthesizer, Copilot as an integrationist, Gemini as a
skeptic, Notion as the documentarian, and Replit as a late, under-briefed
specialty condition. These are contextual roles, not a new scoring taxonomy.
