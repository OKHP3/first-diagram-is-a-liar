## Post copy

Notion wasn't just another model in my Mermaid experiment.

It did draw diagrams. But drawing wasn't its main contribution.

At the time, Notion's built-in AI used lower-cost, earlier-generation models, not the frontier models available elsewhere in the experiment. I didn't treat its diagrams as a like-for-like comparison.

What Notion did better was keep the room's memory.

It helped shape the PRD and requirements, consolidate drafts and model outputs, and record the interviews where I asked each system what it would change and where a competitor had done better. It kept the decisions, revisions, and receipts navigable after the chat windows closed.

In my MCP-connected setup, Notion became the hub. I could prepare the brief once and carry that context to the other platforms instead of repeating myself six times.

It became the assistant I trusted to keep the details straight: part documentarian, part courtroom stenographer, part working memory for the project.

That was a different role from the Core Five. Notion wasn't in a like-for-like diagram contest, and its diagram didn't earn its place by outdrawing the frontier models. Its value was helping the whole experiment remain coherent, inspectable, and reusable.

Notion didn't make the work automatically true. I still had to judge the evidence.

What do you rely on to keep a multi-tool project from losing its memory?
