## Post

One reason this article series took me so long was not time.

It was the mental whirlwind.

Every day I learned something new, went a little deeper down the hole, and found another turn in the maze.

The more I learned about Mermaid, the more captivated I became. Not because it could make cool pictures. Not because I could “vibe diagram.”

Because the words and the diagram could become the same artifact.

For most of my life, diagramming meant translating words into shapes, routing lines, and arranging a canvas. Then the words changed. The process evolved. The diagram fell behind. Eventually both versions became irrelevant.

Mermaid changes that equation. Keep the structure as text, and the visual can be regenerated as the source changes.

That was the first multiplier.

Then business processes exposed the harder problem.

A person's link in the chain is not one symbol and three words. It can contain years of judgment, exceptions, handoffs, workarounds, and consequences. If someone gives you hours of their experience, compressing it into a tidy box can become another kind of lie.

And if the goal is to make that process usable by an AI agent, a diagram is nowhere near enough. You need the narrative, rules, decisions, controls, measures, SOPs, and handoff material behind it.

So BPMN for Mermaid changed shape.

It started as a renderer for a readable, text-first `bpmn-beta` subset. It grew into BP-SKILL: 15 agent skills for collecting, structuring, challenging, documenting, visualizing, and handing off a business process.

The diagram became the happy accident.

The bigger idea is a review loop: conversation becomes structured process documentation; the major steps become a visual model; the person who actually knows the work can see what was captured, challenge it, and correct it while the knowledge is still fresh.

That full near-real-time loop is still a direction, not a finished production system. The public project today is a technical prototype, a bounded BPMN descriptive subset, and a portable 15-skill process-documentation suite.

The renderer still matters.

But the larger offering is helping preserve the human knowledge the diagram is supposed to represent.

See the project and its current boundaries: https://overkillhill.com/projects/bpmn-for-mermaid/
