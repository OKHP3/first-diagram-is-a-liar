## Draft post

The first diagram is usually a liar.

Not because the person who made it is trying to mislead you.

Because the first diagram is usually the moment when a complicated thing first
becomes visible. It shows the shape we hope is true before the loops, forks,
handoffs, exceptions, and bad assumptions have had time to object.

That was the real lesson behind my Mermaid experiment.

It was never just “ask an AI for a diagram.”

The work started with context: a place to keep prompts, decisions, revisions,
and receipts after the chat window closed. Then came protocol: a shared brief,
disclosed conditions, and a way to compare outputs without pretending every
tool had the same role.

Then came the useful part: revision.

The strongest diagrams were not the ones that looked most finished on the first
try. They were the ones that made their own uncertainty visible. Loops. Forks.
Dead ends. The places where an elegant story had to be rebuilt because reality
did not cooperate.

That is structural truth.

And it changes what “good” looks like.

Maintainability matters because a diagram that cannot be traced back to its
source, render, and correction path is just a confident screenshot. Visual
governance matters because once AI makes structure cheap, the next problem is
making related diagrams look like they belong to the same system. Notation
choice matters because a flowchart can describe a process without carrying the
roles, events, gateways, and handoffs that make it a process.

The AI contribution was perspective.

The human contribution was still the job that matters most: set the conditions,
notice the disagreement, decide what the evidence supports, and refuse to call
a clean picture true just because it is clean.

The final rule is delivery.

Keep the Mermaid source. Keep the fallback. Check the links. Label the
referrals. Make the work inspectable when a provider changes, a renderer fails,
or a reader asks, “How do you know?”

That is the Zac Snyder Cut: more context, more receipts, more visible revision,
and a much harder time hiding the parts that make the system real.

The first diagram may still be a liar.

But the next version can become a better witness.
